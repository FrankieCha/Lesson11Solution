Johor-Singapore Special Economic Zone

JS-SEZ guide for Singapore manufacturers

The JS-SEZ offers Singapore manufacturers a pathway to expand capacity, strengthen supply chains and stay competitive. This page summarises the SMF JS-SEZ Playbook.

The JS-SEZ spans over 3,500 km². It has nine zones for investments across 11 sectors.

The nine zones

Each zone has its own targeted economic activity. Companies are free to set up in the zone that best suits their needs. (p. 6)

  • Senai-Skudai

    Aerospace manufacturing and Maintenance, Repair and Overhaul (MRO).

  • Kulai-Sedenak

    AI, quantum technology supply chain, medical devices, pharmaceuticals.

  • Tanjung Pelepas

    Logistics, port operations.

  • Johor Bahru City Centre

    Global services, digital economy.

  • Iskandar Puteri

    Manufacturing, tourism, education.

  • Forest City Special Financial Zone

    Global services, smart logistics, relocation (services).

  • Tanjung Langsat-Kong Kong

    Downstream specialty chemicals.

  • Pengerang Integrated Petroleum Complex

    Oil and gas, petrochemicals.

  • Desaru-Penawar

    Integrated tourism.

The 11 sectors are: business services, digital economy, education, energy, financial services, food security, green economy, healthcare, logistics, manufacturing and tourism. (p. 4)

What has changed on the ground

  • Singapore set up a JS-SEZ Project Office in April 2025. It brings together MTI, EDB and Enterprise Singapore. (p. 5)
  • Malaysia's Invest Malaysia Facilitation Centre-Johor (IMFC-J) has run since February 2025 as a one-stop shop. (p. 5)
  • From January 2025, one transshipment permit from Singapore Customs is enough for land intermodal transshipments. Before, two permits were needed. (p. 5)
  • Passport-free QR code clearance is in place at the land checkpoints of both countries. (p. 5)

The SETUP framework

SETUP guides companies through the main stages of expansion. (pp. 3, 6)

  1. SSelect your zone strategically

    Match your business to the zones above. Read MIDA's tax incentive packages and EnterpriseSG's financial support schemes. (p. 6)

  2. EEvaluate incentives and requirements

    See the incentives section below. Engage the IMFC-J and IRDA for more support. (pp. 6, 11)

  3. TTake the right legal steps

    Choose a business structure: Sdn. Bhd. for most operations, an LLP, or a regional or representative office. Register with Suruhanjaya Syarikat Malaysia (SSM) and get local council business licences. (p. 7)

    Under the Industrial Coordination Act 1975, a manufacturing licence is needed. It applies to firms with shareholders' funds of RM2.5 million and above, or 75 or more full-time employees. Apply through MIDA. (p. 7)

    Open bank accounts. Register for tax and for employee contributions (EPF, SOCSO, HRDF). Meet customs rules with the Royal Malaysian Customs Department. (p. 7)

  4. UUtilise bilateral support

    Use Singapore's JS-SEZ Project Office and Malaysia's IMFC-J for permits, licences and guidance. Reach out to SMF and the Federation of Malaysian Manufacturers (FMM). (p. 7)

  5. PPrepare your talent and supply chain

    Use the improved immigration policies for skilled workers. Build local supply chain partnerships supported by MIDA. Use training partners to get ready for cross-border work. (p. 7)

Incentives and tax watch-outs

Incentives

  • 5% corporate tax for 10 or 15 years for the manufacturing sector. It is for qualifying activities and for companies with capital investment (excluding land) of at least RM500 million and RM1 billion. (p. 3)
  • 5% personal income tax for knowledge workers. (p. 3)
  • Sector-specific incentives, plus grants and facilitation support from both sides. (p. 3)
  • The incentives are zone specific. They mainly apply to manufacturing (such as AI, quantum computing, medical devices, aerospace and specialty chemicals), smart logistics and global services. (p. 15)
  • Some incentives target large projects, at least RM500 million. Smaller investments can still use Malaysia's standard investment incentives. (p. 15)
  • In Singapore, EDB and EnterpriseSG offer grants and incentives. (p. 15)
  • Incentives should follow the operating model, not dictate it. (p. 15)

Support from Singapore

  • Market Readiness Assistance (MRA) Grant: up to 50% support, capped at S$100,000 per company per new market. (p. 27)
  • Enterprise Financing Scheme: SME Working Capital Loan of up to S$500,000 per borrower group. Trade Loan of up to S$10 million per borrower group. (p. 27)
  • Always confirm the latest terms with the agencies. The Singapore entity must keep substantial involvement. (p. 27)

Watch-out: permanent establishment

If staff of the Singapore company often travel to Johor to supervise operations, negotiate deals or make decisions, Malaysia may see a taxable presence. Keep Singapore and Johor activities clearly separate. Avoid decision-making, contract approvals and long stays in Johor. (p. 14)

Watch-out: transfer pricing

Related companies in Singapore and Johor must trade at fair prices. This is the "arm's length principle". Both countries have strict rules and need documents. Larger companies may explore advance pricing arrangements (APAs). (p. 14)

Watch-out: rules of origin and trade data

Wrong origin declarations can lead to retroactive duties, penalties, interest and loss of tariff benefits. Keep HS codes, origin data and duty records consistent on both sides of the border. (pp. 15, 16)

Watch-out: banking and currency

Accounts for foreign-owned firms can take several weeks to open. Talk to banks before incorporation is final. Plan for SGD-MYR currency risk. To use the 5% corporate tax rate, your banking set-up must fit the reporting and audit rules for incentives. (pp. 10, 11)

Passes for staff

Watch-out: staff tax

The JS-SEZ does not override domestic tax rules. Singapore staff on short assignments in Johor remain subject to Malaysian tax law. The Singapore-Malaysia double taxation agreement only helps if conditions are met. Track workdays, locations and the type of work. (p. 17)

  • Employment Pass (EP)

    MIDA approves expatriate posts for the EP. Category I needs a basic salary of RM10,000 or more. Category II is RM5,000 to RM9,999. Category III is RM3,000 to RM4,999. Minimum salaries are revised from 1 June 2026. (p. 28)

    Posts have no quota restrictions. Applications go through the Xpats Gateway System. The Single Window Platform aims to process EP applications in 5 to 15 working days once documents are complete. (p. 28)

  • Key Post and Term Post

    A Key Post is permanent and is a high-level managerial post. A Term Post is temporary for a set period. (p. 28)

  • Professional Visit Pass (PVP)

    A temporary pass for foreign professionals. It covers training, consultancy or project work such as machine installation. It lasts up to 12 months and cannot be extended. (p. 28)

  • Investor Pass

    Introduced in 2025 for business travellers. Stay is up to 12 months, as 6 months at a time with a possible 6-month extension. Apply online through the Xpats Gateway System. (p. 29)
